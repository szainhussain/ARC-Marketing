# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

- **Primary:** owners of small businesses with no marketing team, or one person doing it, who today pay an agency or freelancer or do it themselves. Typical situations: a local business, an online store, a B2B company that needs leads, or anyone launching a product or offer.
- **Also served:** small in-house marketing teams (a lead plus a few people) who need more output and less coordination work, and agencies running ARC across several client workspaces.
- **The job:** keep marketing running every month (plan, create, publish, measure) without spending their week on coordination, while staying in control of what goes out under their name.

## Product Purpose

ARC ("Strategy. Plan. Execution.") is a marketing department as a service. Twelve specialist AI agents (CMO, Marketing Manager, Social, PR & Partnerships, Paid Media, Creative, Research, Customer Insights, SEO & Growth, Key Accounts, Ops & Governance, Record Keeper) run each campaign through one coordinated workflow and produce versioned deliverables: strategy, plan, content, channel calendars, ad structure, reports.

Success: a customer gets agency-level coordinated marketing every month, approves it in minutes rather than managing it, and can see what ran and what it produced.

## Positioning

A coordinated team, not a content generator, and it acts only after approval. ARC can publish and (with connected accounts) spend, but only after a person approves that exact version of the work, and only within caps the customer sets. Editing anything after approval makes a new version and cancels the old approval. Nothing leaves the workspace without that approval.

Secondary claim: the coordination of an agency at a fraction of the cost (the site has a cost comparison against agency/freelancer fees). ARC makes no outcome guarantees.

## Operating Context

- Customers onboard a business profile and a knowledge library (proposed, then approved, entries), then create campaigns or keep always-on monthly campaigns.
- Two control rooms: the Business Command Center (KPIs, campaigns, what needs attention) and the Marketing Operations Center (agents, live task graph, queue, approval inbox, activity).
- Approvals happen per deliverable version. Approved content is exported formatted for Meta/Instagram, Google Ads and TikTok Ads, or sent through Zapier or Make; publication is only marked when the customer confirms it.
- Results are recorded per campaign and rolled into a monthly client report.
- Roles, two-factor sign-in, audit log export, and a 14-day free trial with no card.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Platform metrics (tasks, approvals, AI cost estimates) are kept separate from business metrics (spend, conversions, revenue); business metrics show as "not connected" until a data source exists.
- If no AI provider is configured, a stage fails honestly as unavailable; it never shows invented output.
- Prices are provisional during early access.
- **Brand Kit:** persona, colours, fonts, visual rules and a logo on the business profile. Every agent receives it.
- **Branded exports:** any deliverable, and the monthly report, downloads as Excel, PowerPoint or PDF in the client's colours with their logo.
- **Image generation:** on-brand images for posts, content variants and ads (needs a Gemini key; without one it fails honestly, with a monthly cap per workspace).
- **Web research:** the research agent reads up to 5 public pages listed on the brief and cites each one it used. Without pages, it says no web research is connected.
- **Ask ARC:** a private assistant per member that answers only from this workspace (approved knowledge, deliverables, results, Brand Kit), shows its sources and links to next steps. Monthly cap.
- **Not yet shipped:** ads that launch after approval with a spend cap, batch-approved outreach, and a WordPress.com publishing pilot. Describe these only once they ship.
- Terminology: "approve", "request approval", "export for publishing", "mark as published". Avoid "handoff" and internal jargon in the UI.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: ARC. Tagline: "Strategy. Plan. Execution." Hero line: "Your whole marketing department. Approved by you."
- Voice: plain, direct, honest. Short sentences, everyday words, no hype, no guaranteed results. Say what ARC does not do as clearly as what it does.
- Fonts in use on the live site: Instrument Serif for display headings, Inter for body and UI text, JetBrains Mono for small notes and tags. Changing them is a rebrand decision for the owner.

## Evidence on Hand

- None yet: no customers, testimonials, case studies, logos or results data. ARC is in early access.
- Do not invent testimonials, customer names or logos, usage numbers, benchmarks, or campaign results. Use the product itself (the preview and workflow demo) as the proof.

## Product Principles

1. **Approval is the product.** Every action that leaves the workspace traces to a person approving an exact version. Never blur that line in copy or UI.
2. **Honest over impressive.** Show "not connected", "estimate" and "not built" plainly instead of filling gaps with plausible numbers.
3. **Owner time is the scarce resource.** Default to fewer decisions, clearer next steps and short review flows.
4. **One team, one workflow.** Present the agents as a coordinated department, not a pile of separate tools.
5. **Plain words.** If an owner with no marketing background would not understand a label, rewrite it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: text contrast, labelled form fields (every label paired with its input), keyboard access, and respect for reduced-motion preferences.
